# A.5.5 Contact with authorities

|---|---|

| Control type (ref.) | Preventive / Corrective |

| 2013 mapping | A.6.1.3 |

## Control objective

This control ensures that the organization establishes and maintains contact arrangements with relevant authorities for information security, such as regulators/supervisory bodies, the data protection authority, national incident response bodies, law enforcement, and emergency services (fire/disaster). In normal times these contacts are needed to track changes in laws/regulations and confirm compliance guidance; in a crisis they are needed to report incidents/disasters promptly and obtain assistance. Deciding in advance who to contact, in what situations, and by what means enables a fast response with no reporting delay or missed statutory deadline when an incident occurs.

## Key checkpoints

1. Are the relevant authorities that may need to be contacted (regulators/supervisory bodies, the data protection authority, incident response bodies, law enforcement, emergency services) identified and listed for information security and privacy?
2. For each authority, are the contact person, contact method, and triggering situations (reportable incident types, statutory reporting deadlines) defined in advance?
3. Is the procedure for reporting to authorities in line with the legally required scope/deadline/method for incidents and personal data breaches reflected in the incident response scheme?
4. Are authority contact details and responsible-person information kept current, and managed so that contact is still possible when a person is unavailable?
5. Are records of contact with authorities (reports, requests for assistance, guidance received) kept and used for subsequent improvement?

## Implementation guidance

- Identify the authorities that need to be contacted based on the regulations/laws and incident types applicable to the organization, and maintain a list with the body name, jurisdiction, contact details, and point of contact.
- Define contact criteria and procedures per situation (routine enquiry, incident report, personal data breach notification, disaster/emergency) and state the reporting scope/deadline/method in the incident response plan (such as A.5.24).
- Link statutory reporting obligations (for example, personal data breach notification/reporting deadlines, incident reporting) to incident classification and severity criteria, so that the reporting procedure is triggered automatically when a threshold is exceeded.
- Designate a contact person and a backup, and maintain an emergency contact scheme and details for incidents that need 24/365 response.
- Considering the sensitivity of information shared with authorities, pre-agree the security of the transmission channel (encryption, approval steps) and the scope of sharing, and consolidate external communication through a single point of contact.
- Regularly verify/update contact details and responsible persons, and record actual contact (reports/assistance) history to feed exercises and procedure improvement.

## Evidence

- Contact list/register of relevant authorities (regulators/supervisory bodies, the data protection authority, incident response bodies, law enforcement, emergency services)
- Incident response procedure or emergency contact scheme stating the reporting scope/deadline/method per situation
- Records of reporting to authorities when a personal data breach/security incident occurs (report forms, acknowledgement of receipt)
- Designation document for authority contact persons and their backups
- Periodic verification/update history of contact details, and contact history such as assistance requests/guidance received

## Nonconformity examples

- An incident or personal data breach occurred, but the bodies to report to and the statutory deadlines were not defined in advance, so reporting was delayed or omitted.
- The authority contact list is outdated and does not reflect changes of person/contact details, so contact cannot be made when needed.
- The incident response plan does not include a procedure for reporting to authorities, so at incident time staff do not know whom to report to or how.
- Only a single contact person is designated, so emergency contact and reporting are impossible during their absence or leave.
- Reporting is made past the statutory deadline (for example, the breach notification/reporting deadline), or the fact and history of reporting are not recorded.

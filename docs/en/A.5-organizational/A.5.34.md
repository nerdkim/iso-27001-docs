# A.5.34 Privacy and protection of PII

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Standard | ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A |
| Theme | A.5 Organizational controls |
| Control | A.5.34 Privacy and protection of PII |
| Control type (ref.) | Preventive |
| Security properties (ref.) | Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability |
| ISMS-P mapping | 3.5.1 Disclosure of the personal information processing policy |
| 2013 mapping | 18.1.4 Privacy and protection of personally identifiable information |

## Control objective
This control requires the organization to identify the privacy protection requirements that applicable laws, regulations, and contracts impose on the personally identifiable information (PII) it processes, and to establish the policies, procedures, and accountability needed to meet them across the full lifecycle of PII, from collection through disposal. It calls for safeguarding the rights of data subjects, preventing misuse, leakage, and unauthorized access to PII, and communicating the organization's approach to privacy protection to everyone involved in PII processing. The ultimate aim is to prevent the sanctions and loss of trust that follow breaches of privacy law, and to run PII processing activities in alignment with the organization's management system and risk management.

## Key checkpoints
1. Are the types, processing purposes, and flows of the PII the organization processes identified, and are the applicable privacy-related legal, regulatory, and contractual requirements understood?
2. Is a policy/guideline on privacy and PII protection established, and is it communicated and shared with all personnel and parties involved in PII processing?
3. Is an accountable owner for PII protection (such as a privacy officer) and a point of contact appointed, with roles and authority defined?
4. Are technical and organizational safeguards applied across the PII lifecycle: collection, use, provision, storage, and disposal?
5. Is there a procedure to receive and handle data subject requests such as access, correction, deletion, and suspension of processing?
6. Are privacy risks associated with PII processing, such as those from new services or systems, assessed and reflected in the controls?

## Implementation guidance
- Identify the items, processing purposes, retention periods, processing locations, and third-party provision/outsourcing status of the PII the organization collects and processes, document this as a processing inventory (flow map/record of processing), and keep it current.
- Determine the applicable privacy laws, regulations, and contractual requirements (including domestic and foreign jurisdictions) in conjunction with the compliance requirements register in A.5.31, and separately manage requirements for cross-border transfers such as transfer abroad.
- Establish a policy/guideline on privacy and PII protection that defines processing principles, roles and responsibilities, procedures for upholding data subject rights, and safeguard standards, and communicate it to employees, processors, and external parties involved in PII processing.
- Appoint an accountable owner for PII protection and a point of contact (privacy officer/responsible function), and operate a procedure to receive and handle inquiries, rights requests, and breach reports.
- Design and apply technical and organizational safeguards for each stage of the PII lifecycle, such as data minimization, least-privilege access, encryption/pseudonymization/masking, access logging, and disposal once the retention period expires.
- Conduct a privacy (impact) assessment when introducing new services/systems or when processing PII at scale, to identify and mitigate risks, and keep the processing policy and related notices up to date.
- Establish and operate procedures for handling data subject rights requests (access, correction, deletion, suspension of processing, withdrawal of consent) and for notification/reporting in the event of a personal data breach.

## Evidence
- PII processing inventory (processing items/purposes/retention periods/processing locations/third-party provision and outsourcing status, including flow diagrams)
- Privacy and PII protection policy/guideline and records of its communication/training to employees and processors
- Appointment document for the privacy officer and point of contact, and a definition of roles and responsibilities
- Results of privacy (impact) assessments and records of follow-up action
- Personal information processing policy and records of data subject notices/consent
- Register of receipt and handling of data subject rights requests (access, correction, deletion, suspension of processing)
- Evidence of applied PII safeguards (encryption/pseudonymization/access privileges/access logs/disposal records)

## Nonconformity examples
- The types and flows of PII the organization processes are not identified, so it cannot determine what personal data exists and where.
- No policy or guideline on privacy/PII protection exists, or where one exists it is not communicated to the personnel/processors who actually handle PII.
- No privacy officer or point of contact is appointed, so responsibility for responding to data subject inquiries and rights requests is unclear.
- The applicable privacy laws or cross-border transfer requirements are not identified, so legal requirements are not met.
- PII whose retention period has expired is not disposed of but retained, or it is used/provided beyond the notified purpose.
- No procedure exists to receive and handle data subject rights requests such as access and deletion, so requests cannot be answered.
- No privacy risk assessment is performed for large-scale PII processing or the introduction of new systems.
